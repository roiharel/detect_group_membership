Clustering, and the three ways a boundary opens

Figure 1. How positions become social structure, and what the three kinds of boundary opening look like in the record. (a) Clustering in space, one hour of two units, 06 Dec 2025, 13:00. Each animal is drawn as its fix at that hour with a tail of its preceding 6 hourly fixes, filled by the unit it is travelling with, so the clusters can be read against how the animals were actually moving. Dashed outlines enclose the animals the clustering placed in one spatial cluster, blue where a cluster holds both units, and numbers give cluster sizes. 30 animals fall into 5 clusters, of which 0 holds both units, with a nearest cross-unit distance of 1623 m. A ring marks an animal whose origin group is not the unit it is travelling with — 2 here, from Chartreuse — so the individual axis and the group axis appear in the same frame. The scale bar shows the distance the clustering is acting on rather than asking the reader to take the 120–900 m parameter on trust. The frame is a daytime hour so that the tails are a continuous track; a tail spanning the night would be one straight jump between sleep sites, since collars do not fix between 17:00 and 02:00. Inset: the same machinery over time for one animal — 24AC17 of Chartreuse, drawn at the same pixels-per-hour as (b) and over the first day of the same window, for one of the animals in (b)’s own rows — so the two hourly strips can be read against each other directly. The upper bar is social context, recomputed every hour and taking 4 distinct values inside that single day; the lower bar is the dynamic unit, which changes only on seven days of sustained association and takes 1. Extending the same animal to 14 days — too wide to draw at this scale, since a fortnight needs about 4,700 px — gives 5 contexts against 1 dynamic label across 337 hours. Membership is deliberately far more stable than the situation it summarises. (b) Every pattern at once, Copper - PhantomWest, 2026-07-11; 172 h in a shared cluster, 52 split off, 25 alone. Rather than one panel per event type, this is a single 46-hour window over two units in which all three co-occur, so they can be read against each other rather than across separate examples. Rows are collared animals with the rule separating the two units; columns are consecutive hours across two day-night cycles; and every animal shown has a known state in every hour, so the raster carries no gaps. Grey is an animal with the bulk of its own unit, bronze an animal split off into a secondary cluster of its own unit, blue a cluster holding both units, and slate an animal alone. The three level-one events are therefore the three departures from grey: a within-group split is a bronze block, a between-group merge a blue one, and isolation a slate cell. On scale. Hourly resolution shows the transitions but not the outcomes. Median durations are 2 h for a within-group split and 1 h for isolation, so those are fully contained in a window this size; the median between-group encounter spans 14 h and the median individual excursion 2 nights, with tails reaching months, so this window catches one event out of a longer sequence. Individual excursions are shown separately for that reason.

On the overnight assumption. The export already encodes it as is_carried_night, and carried rows retain social_context, association_event_id and temp_group_size in 100% of cases — so both the location and the nature of the interaction carry. Treating fixed-or-carried as known raises coverage from 58.7% of animal-hours to 96.3%. One hole remains and is excluded here: 02:00, the handover hour, where the carry-forward has ended and the morning fix has not happened (27% known). Note what the assumption does and does not buy: it makes durations continuous across nights, but a carried hour is not an independent observation, so it should not be counted as new evidence in anything that rests on observation counts. Candidate windows were scored on completeness before event properties: for each candidate the search finds the animals with a known state in every interval and keeps the window yielding the most, requiring at least 4 per unit (9 for the within-group panel). This replaces a six-type taxonomy that split merges into large, medium-partial and small-subset — that subdivision rests on a classifier keyed to the maximum participation a unit ever reaches rather than its typical state, and it does not distinguish groups (between-group ICC 0.05–0.13, permutation p = 0.09–0.26). Carried as the continuous depth in (a, b) it loses nothing. All panels and depth distributions come from the frozen 1,924,104-row export (2024-03-01 to 2026-07-22); within-group depth uses unit-hours with at least 8 observed animals.
