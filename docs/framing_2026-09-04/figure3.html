Three routes across a group boundary

Figure 3. The three ways a group boundary becomes fluid, shown (a–b) and then compared (c–d). Colour is constant: ochre within-group modularity and splits, blue group meetings, plum individual transfer. Intervals are 95% cluster bootstraps (1200 replicates) on the natural unit of replication — unit, dyad or animal — never on the row. (a) Lilac week by week, 2025-07-28 to 2026-01-31, over the weeks it carried 12 or more collars. 18 of the 27 weeks shown are modular, in 4 shaded phases of two weeks or more, the longest 8 consecutive weeks, reaching 0.41 at most and dividing into as many as 3 communities, the largest holding as little as 44% of the group. The two insets are that group’s own weeks, placed where the series leaves room and ruled back to the point each one illustrates: at Q = 0.41 (17 animals) it is two communities that barely associate; at Q = 0.14 the same two are still there but so heavily bridged that the group is nearly one piece — which is what the lower half of this scale means, and it is not the same thing as a group with no partition at all. The axis stops at 2026-01-31 for legibility; the record runs on to 2026-07-13, and in those further 24 weeks modularity never exceeds 0.06 (20 modular weeks of 51 across the whole record). Across the 12 well-covered units, 10 reach modularity in some week and 2 never leave zero, but Chartreuse and Jade hold 57 of the 94 modular weeks (61%). (b) Both between-unit networks on one node layout, node area by median collar count. Blue chords are group meetings (62 dyads over 26 units, 2,053 bouts, width by hours together); plum chords are animals moving (29 directed edges, 273 excursions by 48 animals, 8 of the edges reciprocal, the heaviest Chartreuse to Emerald carrying 34). A meeting requires two or more animals on both sides: 919 dyad-days (24% of all detected encounters, 65 dyads) had a single animal on one side and count as transfer, not meeting — their group centroids sit a median 4,222 m apart against 30 m for a real meeting. That separation is what makes the overlay readable: 42 dyads meet without ever exchanging an animal, 5 exchange without a recorded meeting, and 20 do both — meeting and exchanging are largely different relationships between the same units. (c) Concentration, four quantities against one diagonal with Gini and its interval in the panel. Encounter-hours per dyad is the most unequal (0.81 across 62 dyads), modular weeks per unit next (0.59); excursions spread more evenly, over 29 transfer edges (0.59) and 48 animals (0.64, dashed). (d) Duration, all three at hourly resolution. 95% of meetings end within a day and 43% within six hours (median 13 h); splits are shorter still (3 h, 98% inside a day), which is how a group can be modular for seven consecutive weeks without being apart for a whole day. The excursion curve is an uninterrupted away-state run rather than a whole dispersal — a long absence is cut every time the hourly context returns to origin — so the audited nightly view (338 excursions, 22% past a week, out to 500 nights) remains the measure of how long leaving lasts. The shaded strip below 2 h is a resolution floor: 35% of splits and 40% of excursions are single hours against 14% of meetings, because one animal's cluster membership flickers where a whole group's co-occurrence does not.

Panel (a) is restricted to well-covered unit-weeks: below about 12 collars a modularity estimator returns a single community regardless of the animals. Its two networks are matched on collar count, so the flat one cannot be dismissed as a thinner week; an edge is the share of hours two animals shared an association cluster among hours both were present, drawn at 0.05 and above, communities are greedy modularity communities, and positions come from a spring layout with each community’s centre placed on a fixed circle, so distance on the page is indicative rather than metric. Panels (b) and (c) are not coverage-restricted, which is why node area carries coverage instead. The two chord types in (b) are mostly but not entirely independent evidence: 16 dyads carry both, and on Copper–Lilac (84% of encounter hours) and Maroon–Sapphire (91%) most of the meeting record falls inside a period when one of their animals was living in the other unit, so those two should not be cited as evidence that meeting and exchanging are separate channels. Transfer destinations are the modal other unit sharing the focal animal’s clusters on that night; 86.3% of joined away-nights resolve and the rest are left unassigned, so (c) is a lower bound. All three curves in (e) come from derive_level2_inputs.py at hourly resolution with gaps of up to two hours bridged, the excursion curve held to the 91 animals of the audited axis-C cohort. Weekly metrics come from the frozen 1,924,104-row export. Generated by build_figure3.py; seed 20260904.
