Event composition varies widely across groups

Figure 3. Event-type composition across 23 social groups. Rows are groups, ordered by the leftmost proportion; the number beside each group name is its event total. Dots are observed proportions, horizontal lines 95% percentile bootstrap intervals over that group's own events (2,000 draws); the dashed line in each column is the pooled proportion across all groups. (a) Family composition. Pooled, 38% of events are between-group merges, 27% within-group splits and 35% individual separations — but the spread is wide: LapisSplinter is 58% merges against Jade at 19%, while Jade produces 41% splits. (b) Composition of the merges themselves, pooled over the record. The spread is wide — PhantomWest is 84% small subsets of 163 merges, FireOpal 82% large of 71 — but this ordering does not survive a temporal decomposition and should be read as variation in the population rather than as differences between groups. See Figure 4: once quarter-to-quarter drift is retained, merge-scale composition has an ICC of 0.05–0.13 and is not distinguishable from shuffled group labels (permutation p = 0.09–0.26), and well-sampled groups traverse most of the range within their own record. (c) Observed between-group spread (dot) against the mean of a multinomial null in which each group draws its own number of events from the pooled distribution (grey bar), in three collar-coverage strata. Family composition varies at 8.8× the null across all groups and 8.0× within a matched 7–14 collar band; merge-scale composition at 3.9× and 4.6× respectively. The variation is not a coverage artefact — it strengthens when coverage is matched. Note the limit of this null, though: it assumes each group draws from a fixed pooled distribution with no temporal structure, so it tests whether the spread exceeds sampling, not whether groups differ. Figure 4 applies the stronger test.

Merges are dyadic and counted for both participating groups, so merge counts sum to twice the merge event total; splits and individual separations are attributed to the origin group. Groups with fewer than 30 events are omitted. Collar coverage does bias detection by type — the within-group-split share correlates with median collars at Spearman +0.41 and the merge share at -0.32, as expected since a split needs two clusters visible while a merge needs one animal from each side — which is why panel (c) repeats the test inside coverage strata. Events and exposure both come from the legacy hourly source filtered to 2025-01-01 onward; cohort reconciliation with the frozen export is outstanding.
