03

Duration: every structure is brief, and every structure has a tail

Survival curves — the share of events still running past a given duration — on a log axis. The project held 181 saved figures and not one cumulative distribution, which is how five structures with medians between 1 and 11 hours and maxima between 217 and 6,330 hours came to be summarised by their medians alone.

Common, brief, and heavy-tailed — at every scale. The median says the structures are transient; the tail says a handful are not, and those are the ones that reach real mixing.

06

What the census establishes on its own

Nothing here needs a model. Four statements follow from the counts and the curves alone, and all four are population-scale.

1. Group identity persists while configuration varies constantly. 82.7% of nights with the origin group; 33.2% of nights in a configuration that is not the whole group. Both true of the same 81,695 nights.

2. Every structure is brief and heavy-tailed. Medians of 1–11 h across five event types, maxima of 217–6,330 h. No structure is typically long; every structure occasionally is.

3. Duration is not one quantity. Span, exposure and contact differ by about 53-fold on the same events. Any result that reported one as another inherited the error.

4. Depth tracks duration, not the reverse. Alone-only excursions last one night; excursions reaching another unit last three and are the only ones with mass past the settlement threshold.

What the census cannot establish: seven of the ten structures in §2 have no denominator, so their counts are inventories. Whether they are common in the population — as opposed to common in this collar deployment — is a question the census cannot answer and the models in the companion pages have to.
