Every social structure is brief, and duration is not one quantity

Figure 2. Duration distributions of social structures in a 350-animal, 26-group baboon cohort. Curves are complementary cumulative distributions, P(duration > x); shaded bands are 95% percentile intervals from a cluster bootstrap (1,000 draws) resampling the natural unit of replication — dyads for merges, groups for within-group splits, animals for individual structures. (a) Six structures, log duration axis. Every structure has a median at or below 11 h and a tail beyond one month: large merge median 11 h (max 564 h, 46 dyads), within-group split 2 h (max 2637 h, 24 groups), single-animal separation 1 h (max 6330 h, 301 animals). Small subset merges are the least frequent but the most persistent, crossing above large merges beyond roughly 30 h. (b) The same 1,705 between-group encounters measured three ways: structural span (median 14 h, n = 1,705), fine-scale supported exposure (2.30 h, n = 751) and active cross-group contact (0.27 h, n = 571) — a 53-fold separation between the outermost two, on identical events. The falling sample reflects fine-scale coverage, not shorter encounters. (c) Individual excursions by the depth reached. Excursions that only reach being alone (median 1 night, n = 163) are shorter than those reaching another social unit (4 nights, n = 175); the curves separate immediately and do not re-cross, and only the latter carries appreciable mass beyond the 7-night threshold that defines settlement.

Panel (a) derives from the legacy hourly inventory filtered to 2025-01-01 onward; panels (b) and (c) from the frozen 1,924,104-row export (2024-03-01 to 2026-07-22). Cohort reconciliation is outstanding for (a). Curves are distinguished by dash pattern as well as hue so the figure reproduces in greyscale.
