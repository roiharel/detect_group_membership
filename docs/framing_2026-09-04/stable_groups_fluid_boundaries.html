General framing · supersedes PAPER_STRUCTURE_GENERAL

Stable groups, fluid boundaries

Three routes across a group boundary, measured on one cohort — and, for each one, what a 350-animal, 26-group, 29-month dataset actually licenses.

1,924,104 animal-hours 81,695 animal-nights 350 animals 26 origin groups 2024-03-01 → 2026-07-22
01

The thesis

A group is not a container. It is a baseline animals return to, and the interesting quantity is not whether the baseline exists but how, how often, and how deeply animals leave it.

On 82.7% of 81,695 animal-nights an animal sleeps with its origin group. That is the stability. On the other 17.3% it is somewhere else — and on 33.2% of nights the configuration it sleeps in is not its whole group: merged with another group, split from part of its own, or alone. Stability and fluidity are properties of the same population at the same time, measured on the same rows.

The paper’s job is to turn that pair of facts into a general, quantified statement about which routes across a group boundary are used, how far each one goes, and what a population-scale dataset can establish about each.

02

Three routes across the same boundary

A group boundary can become fluid in three ways, and the frozen cohort measures all three on the same rows. They are not three studies but three measurements of one quantity — boundary permeability — taken at different grain: the group crosses, the group’s internal structure divides, or an individual crosses alone.

Isolation and dispersal are one axis, not two. An animal away from its origin group is either alone in the landscape or with another group, and those are graded depths of a single individual-level process — see §4.3. Held apart, they produced a 285-animal isolation inventory that was roughly three-quarters observation artefact alongside a 17-animal dispersal case series that could not carry a rate. Merged and support-filtered, they give one axis with 91 animals, 338 excursions and an explicit denominator.

Axis A Between-group interaction & mixture

Two groups occupy the same space; individuals may or may not mix inside it. Whole groups cross first, then individuals within the merged mass.

25.1% of animal-nights
Axis B Within-group fission & modularity

A group divides into subunits that may or may not have persistent membership. What crosses is the group’s own internal structure — measurable only above about twelve collars.

4,570 splits, 24 groups
Axis C Individual excursion

One animal leaves the origin group, reaching either nothing (alone) or another unit (joined), then returns or settles. Isolation and dispersal are depths of this one process, not separate axes.

338 excursions, 91 animals

The axes interact, and the interactions point the same way

  • LapisSplinter is a splinter unit that supplies the most permeable dyad on axis A: Lapis–LapisSplinter has the smallest deficit of the twelve measurable dyads (−0.295), LapisSplinter–Magenta the fourth smallest (−0.356), and LapisSplinter appears in two of the five dyads that ever entered sustained association. It also receives the one genuine transfer in that lineage (24AC02_3C4D, origin Lapis, 289 days into its own record). But the fission itself is not in the record: six of LapisSplinter’s seven animals carry origin_group = LapisSplinter and were all first observed on the day they were collared, 2024-11-11, when Lapis’s own collars jumped from 6 to 14. The unit name encodes field knowledge of a split that predates tracking. A parent and its splinter mix more than any other pair — that is the observation; fission dynamics are not.
  • Sapphire is the only group with substantial median within-group modularity (0.278, axis B) and it is one of three dyads with a sustained association on axis A (Maroon–Sapphire).
  • Axis C is the raw material. 5,828 single-animal separation events across 301 of 350 animals are what both a brief absence and a permanent dispersal are cut from. The classifier that separates them is the weakest link in the project.
Prediction the framing generates — and why this dataset cannot test it

Splinter units and internally structured groups are the most permeable units here. But the one candidate fission was not observed: Lapis carried 4–6 collars across all 18 of its pre-2024-11-11 group-weeks, below the twelve-collar modularity threshold, so “did Lapis show modular phases before it divided?” is untestable — not negative. Answering it needs a group tracked with twelve or more collars through a division. State the prediction; do not present the data as adjudicating it.

03

What population-level coverage buys

This is the general argument, and it is worth stating as a method contribution rather than burying it. Population coverage does three specific things no case study can do, and each has already changed a conclusion here.

3.1 — It converts a case into a distribution with a documented tail

Before: Copper–Lilac fused, therefore group boundaries are permeable. After: twelve measurable dyads all mix far below composition expectation (dyad-weighted −0.405 [−0.442, −0.367], 12 of 12 negative), and Copper–Lilac’s encounters rank tenth of twelve. The general result is stronger than the case ever was, and the case became the labelled endpoint.

3.2 — It separates an entity from a state

This is the most valuable thing coverage does, and it generalises across all three axes. With one dyad you cannot tell whether an extreme value is a property of that pair or of a condition the pair happened to be in. With twelve you can: Copper–Lilac’s discrete encounters give −0.339 (inside the pack); its sustained association gives −0.033 (near expectation). The outlier is a state, not a dyad — and five dyads entered that state.

If the answer is “state” on all three axes, the paper has a single general finding rather than three descriptive chapters.

3.3 — It makes negatives real

Coverage is what lets an observed absence be distinguished from a missing row. The opportunity table holds 70,312 observed-no-encounter dyad-days with a median 24 shared observed hours and a median closest approach of 10.8 km, against 2,867 detected-encounter days with a median closest approach of 66 m. The saved pipeline had zero-filled 2,406 real encounters across 53 dyads as non-events, with no exposure measure behind any zero.

Axis C now has the same thing — 82,205 animal-nights in four explicit states, with unresolvable named rather than silently scored as isolation (§4.3). Axis B still has none: there is no opportunity table for a group failing to split, so every rate there is an event count without a denominator.

Axis A — best supported, and the model is fitted

Two-stage separation works. Stage 1 (72,724 dyad-days, 2,852 encounters, 3.92%): whether groups meet is driven by geometry and by history — prior encounters carry OR 1.762 [1.486, 2.088] after both between-dyad and lag-1 within-dyad distance are in the model, and OR 1.57 [1.27, 1.93] in the restricted sample where the groups were already within 5 km yesterday. Encounter propensity is a dyad property: dyad-effect SD 1.58 against shared-group-effect SD 0.044, stable across three prior scales. Out-of-sample AUC with whole dyads held out is 0.880; the calibration slope is 0.540, a stated limit.

Stage 2 (738 discrete encounters, 12 dyads): the odds of a cross-group link during a discrete encounter are 4.6% of the composition expectation (intercept −3.073 [−3.433, −2.765]). Duration is the only robust predictor (log span +0.624 [+0.302, +0.828]). Nothing that predicts meeting predicts mixing.

What coverage forbids on axis A

Any between-dyad statement at Stage 2. Twelve of 68 structural dyads have fine-scale support. Every Stage 2 result is within-dyad, and the paper must say so in the results text, not only in limitations.

Axis B — modularity is measurable above twelve collars, and it is a state

The event side is genuinely general: 4,570 split events across 24 groups, median duration 2 h, p90 14 h, median maximum split fraction 0.25, p90 0.50. Splits are overwhelmingly binary — 2 clusters in 3,829 events (83.8%), 3 in 628, four or more in 113 — and shallow: the typical split detaches a quarter of the observed animals for two hours.

Modularity is where coverage bites, and the threshold is sharp. Detectability scales monotonically with collar count, so nothing can be compared across groups until coverage is matched:

Cohort check — gap 7.3, now closed for these metrics

The weekly metrics were rebuilt from the frozen export, matching the legacy definition (11:00 and 16:00 UTC sampling, observed positions only, cluster identity from association_event_id): 1,268 group-weeks in 23 units against the legacy 1,418 in 26. What holds: the modularity–collar relationship (0.222 vs 0.225), the between-unit ICC (0.169 vs 0.148, both on 12 units), the null collar term above threshold, Lilac (55.3% vs 53.8% of weeks modular; max modularity 0.449 vs 0.458), week-to-week stickiness (Lilac 0.417 vs 0.413; Chartreuse 0.399 vs 0.331), and the cohesive units at 0.0%. What does not: the split-detection confound above, and Chartreuse, which falls from 45.0% of 20 weeks to 14.7% of 34. Lilac is the one unit whose recurrent modular phase replicates across cohorts. The axis-B NDVI models below still run on the legacy weekly file and are the remaining piece of 7.3.

But modularity is mostly a state, not a group property. A random-intercept model on the well-covered group-weeks (≥12 collars, 12 units) puts the between-unit ICC at 0.169 on the frozen cohort and 0.148 on the legacy one — roughly 85% of the variance is within group across time, on both sources. It is a sticky state rather than noise: lag-1 week-to-week autocorrelation is 0.417 in Lilac and 0.399 in Chartreuse on the frozen cohort (0.413 and 0.331 legacy), against roughly zero in Lapis, Maroon, Purple and RubyRunners. A group enters a modular phase and stays in it for weeks.

That gives Result 5 its third leg: extreme permeability is a state, not an entity, now shown on all three axes with three different measurements.

Environmental conditions do not predict when a group splits, or for how long

Group-weeks are axis B’s opportunity table: a group-week with observation support and no split is an observed negative. 836 group-weeks in 19 units join to Sentinel-2 NDVI. Three readings. The within-group NDVI term is null in six of eight specifications, and in the two strictest it turns negative with the interval barely excluding 1 — greener-than-usual weeks give marginally fewer substantial splits, if anything. The large between-group term is not evidence of anything ecological: it is range greenness across 19 units, inseparable from group identity, and it is the same failure mode as axis A’s seasonal term, which fell from OR 0.62 [0.44, 0.86] to 0.86 [0.69, 1.07] under restriction. Collar coverage predicts split detection below twelve collars and stops above it — which independently validates the well-covered threshold.

Split extent given a split week (596 weeks) has both NDVI terms null and only collar coverage surviving (1.132 [1.063, 1.206]): as currently defined, extent measures observation, not behaviour. Split duration (655 events, 14 units; median 2 h, p90 13 h, max 240 h) gives a within-group NDVI coefficient of +0.051 [+0.002, +0.099] on log hours — marginal, and pointing the opposite way from the occurrence result, while the collar term is five times larger at +0.237 [+0.054, +0.420].

Verdict on the ecological prediction

No robust environmental effect on fission — not on whether a group splits, not on how much of the week it spends split, not on how long a split lasts. Two marginal within-group signals with opposite signs are not a finding. This retires the old Phase 5 prediction — scarcity fragments, abundance connects — on the fission side as written. Report it as tested and unsupported, which is more useful than leaving it an unstated ambition. NDVI is not the whole hypothesis, though: the Sentinel-2 product also carries NDWI, LSWI and EVI, and water availability is a different mechanism that has not been tested.

What coverage still forbids on axis B

Modularity claims below twelve collars, and any unadjusted split rate — detection correlates with collar count at Spearman 0.489. The matched Chartreuse–Purple comparison remains a clean null (difference 0.0015 [−0.025, 0.023]) and should be reported as one. And this axis still runs on the legacy source filtered to 2025-01-01, while axes A and C are on the frozen export from 2024-03-01 — gap 7.3 is now the last structural blocker here.

A result that qualifies the premise

The reason to merge was that a dispersing animal either moves alone or joins another group — two phases of one process. The data says they are mostly not sequential: only 11 of 338 excursions (3%) contain both states, rising to 15 of 196 under generous gap bridging and 10% under the permissive nightly rule. Floating alone is rarely the route into another group — animals that end up elsewhere mostly appear there directly. On separate axes the question could not be asked at all.

The sparse-collar artefact, and the rule that removes it

With few collars an animal can be scored isolated by accident — when it is the only tracked animal in its group, or when a two-collar group splits and both sides look alone. The fix is a single principle: a social state is assignable only when the data could have shown a different state. For alone, the origin group has to be locatable somewhere else at the same moment.

An animal-hour is scored alone or with_other only if (1) the focal animal’s own position is observed, not carried, and (2) at least two collared animals of its origin group are observed in the same hour, co-clustered with each other, in a cluster the focal animal is not in. Condition 2 is a located reference group — “I can see your group over there, and you are not in it” — not a partner count.

The rule removes exactly the cases it was built for, by construction rather than by tuning — with one collar there is never a reference cluster, and with two collars apart neither side reaches size two:

Two definitional choices that must be pinned, not defaulted

The nightly-state rule moves the excursion count tenfold. dominant (the night takes its most frequent hourly state) gives 338 excursions in 91 animals, 92.0% of nights resolvable; any_away (away if any hour was away) gives 3,541 in 303 animals, 95.3% resolvable — but 71% of those are single nights, so it is largely counting brief daytime departures. Recommend dominant as primary.

Gap tolerance matters under dominant and not under any_away. Bridging unresolvable nights collapses 338 excursions to 234 / 210 / 199 / 196 at gaps of 1 / 2 / 3 / 7, stabilising from gap 3; settlement candidates fall from 61 to 22 as trips merge. Under any_away it moves nothing (3,541 → 3,534). The animal count — 91, or 303 — never moves on either rule at any gap, so the sample is stable and only the event parsing is sensitive.

What coverage still forbids on axis C

The 285-animal isolation figure and both dispersal inventories (28 events / 9 animals; 71 segments / 17 animals) should be labelled legacy and not reported again. Excursion outcomes and censoring are not yet assigned — the 500-night Chartreuse excursion is still open at the end of the record. And the night definition here (a window_start − 10 h calendar cut, 82,205 animal-nights) must be reconciled with the canonical 16:00–06:00 night (81,695) before any rate is published.

5.2 — Depth and frequency are separately regulated

On axis A this is a positive, fitted result: geometry and history predict whether groups meet; neither predicts how much they mix, and duration is the only thing that does. The corresponding dissociation on axis B — splits happen often; who ends up with whom is weakly determined, ARI 0.100 — is the same pattern at a smaller scale. Two axes, two grains, one dissociation: that is a general claim about fission–fusion regulation, and the strongest broad-audience angle in the project.

5.3 — Depth requires duration, and duration is rare

The only route to near-complete mixing observed anywhere in the population is sustained association: +1.376 [+0.338, +1.543] logit units closer to expectation than the same dyads’ discrete encounters. It occurred in 16 of 1,705 encounters, in 5 of 68 dyads, and only one dyad carried it to near-completion.

The individual axis says the same thing independently. Excursions reaching only alone last a median 1 night; those reaching another unit last 4; settlement takes 7 or more, and 61 of 338 excursions get there. Two scales, two mechanisms, two samples, one pattern: permeability is not a property units have or lack, it is a threshold almost nothing crosses.

5.4 — Opportunity is seasonal; the crossing decision is not

In plain terms

Groups move around the landscape over the year, following the vegetation, so if you simply count how often two groups end up in the same place it looks seasonal. That part is geography. Condition on the days when two groups were already close enough to meet and the pattern disappears — whether they meet has no annual rhythm.

But meeting is not the same as how they meet, and that is a separate question at every scale from a few hundred metres down to two. How much of each group actually joins a merge varies a great deal — from about 62% to 98%. Whether that variation is seasonal is something this record cannot answer: the months with the lowest participation are sampled in a single year, and the same calendar month differs between years by as much as the apparent cycle.

How closely they mix once merged, at 5 m or 2 m, and how much of that structure survives tightening the radius, show no seasonal pattern at all.

Bottom line: the calendar decides where the animals are. It does not decide whether they meet, or how closely they mix once they do. How much of a group turns up to a merge does vary — but 1.6 years of data cannot tell a season from one unusual autumn.

The environment in this system cycles strongly: NDVI deviation from each unit’s own mean has an amplitude of 0.046 peaking on day 199, about 18 July, with joint Wald p < 0.0001 over 7,423 group-days in 23 units. So a null on any axis cannot be explained away by there being no season to respond to.

Before any of it: observation effort rises 121-fold across the record — from 2 collared animals in 1 unit to 242 in 23 — with nightly resolvability going from 0.00 to 0.95. The well-observed window starts 2024-12-01 and contains only 1.53–1.63 seasonal cycles. Season and effort are near-uncorrelated in that window (|r| ≤ 0.12, max VIF 1.25), so a seasonal coefficient is not an effort artefact — but it is estimated from fewer than two cycles, and no interval fixes that.

Only axis A shows a seasonal signal at all, and it looks convincing: amplitude 0.285 logit, peak 12 July, joint p = 0.0103, unchanged by the effort term, and landing within six days of the NDVI peak. Then it fails the one restriction that matters.

There is an annual cycle in where groups are, and none in whether they interact once co-located. Ranges shift with the vegetation cycle, which is exactly why the unrestricted peak tracks NDVI to within a week — the phase match was a property of the geometry, not the behaviour.

The apparent secular decline in encounter probability goes the same way. Unrestricted, the trend is OR 0.748 [0.615, 0.909]; on a balanced panel of the 45 dyads observed throughout it is OR 1.100 [0.884, 1.368] — null, sign flipped. The mechanism is visible in the denominator: dyads in it rise from 45 to 210 as units are collared, and the median closest approach across all dyad-days rises from 8,670 m to 11,437 m. The decline is the denominator filling with distant pairs.

Axis C shows nothing on either response, with or without effort, and that is the most informative null of the three because it has the cleanest denominator in the project.

Down the gradient: participation, mixture, within-merge structure

Collapsing “how they interacted” into one number — the 5 m cross-group contact fraction — is too thin. Once two groups share a spatial cluster at a scale of hundreds of metres, what happens inside spans a gradient, and each level is a different social question. A merge can be large but structurally segregated, or small but thoroughly mixed. Power also decays down that gradient, which is itself a result: 66 dyads at the cluster scale, 12 at 5 m and 2 m, one for betweenness.

And then it fails — for a reason about the record, not the animals

The window holds 1.63 annual cycles, and they are not evenly spread across the calendar. January–July and December appear in two years; August, September, October and November appear in one (2025) — and those are precisely the low-participation months. The fitted trough is a single autumn.

Two checks, both fatal. Refit on months with two or more years of data (1,193 events, 60 dyads): joint p = 0.511. Season demeaned within dyad: participation p = 0.169, large-merge p = 0.116. And the same calendar month differs between years by up to 0.236 in participation — as large as the apparent seasonal range. December alone makes the point: 0.643 in 2024 against 0.880 in 2025.

So the participation signal is a single-year late-2025 feature. Report it as documented variation in how much of a group joins a merge — not as a season. The borderline modularity signals share the same October phase and the same limitation.

One further level is measurable in one dyad only. Betweenness within a merge — who brokers, and how concentrated brokerage is — exists for Copper–Lilac (38 animals, 77 weeks, radii 1/2/5/20 m). Its weekly series looks strongly seasonal at every radius, amplitude falling from 1.74 at 1 m to 0.53 at 20 m. But it is one dyad passing through a fusion, clustered on seven quarter-blocks, and the source metadata records that its fusion hours saturate after 2025-08-01, so a secular transition would alias with season. Not reportable as seasonality — and the reason the within-merge brokerage question stays open is gap 7.5, the 12-of-68 fine-scale coverage.

Depth and duration, not only occurrence

Every test above uses an occurrence response — does an encounter happen, does a split happen, does an animal leave. Depth and duration are different questions, and there was a specific reason to expect them to differ: on two axes duration is the thing that carries depth, so a season could reach depth through duration with occurrence flat. If season acts on depth anywhere, it acts through duration — and 716 events in 12 dyads cannot resolve it. That is an open question, not a null.

Where groups are follows the year. Whether they meet once close does not. How much of each group joins a merge varies widely, and this record cannot say whether that variation is seasonal — the months where participation is lowest are sampled in a single year. How closely they mix once merged shows no seasonal pattern at any radius tested.

The middle clause is the correction. An earlier version of this section read “opportunity is seasonal, the crossing decision is not,” which was reached by collapsing everything downstream of a merge into one 5 m number. The type of interaction is a separate question from the fact of meeting, it does vary substantially, and the honest statement is that 1.6 annual cycles cannot resolve whether the variation is seasonal.

That belongs with Result 4 rather than as a separate ecological finding. Frequency and depth already had different predictors; the same dissociation now appears in time. And the nulls are the more robust half of these results: a null from a 1.6-cycle window is a weak claim of absence, whereas a positive from one is easily an artefact — which is exactly what the single positive turned out to be.

Claim, coverage, verdict

Rare is not the same as unmeasurable

Two things are easy to conflate and must not be. A measurement failure is a number that does not measure what it claims — it would not become true with more data of the same kind. A rare pattern is a sound observation of an uncommon state. Suppressing the second hides the most informative part of a population-scale dataset.

This is 26 social units over 29 months in one landscape — a subset of a population and a subset of the conditions it experiences. Within that subset, every axis shows states only a few units enter. The frequencies are low and are reported as such. But the presence of a tail in a 26-unit sample is itself the result: the population contains variation in social context, and this sample is too small to have exhausted it. More groups across a wider environmental range would sample that tail better; they would not make it disappear.

That also places the nulls. The seasonal and environmental nulls say that within the range this sample covers, no annual or vegetation signal organises boundary crossing. They do not say no such signal exists across a wider environmental range.

The verdict column below is therefore three-way: report supported at population scale, report as variation rare but soundly measured, and do not report measurement failure.
