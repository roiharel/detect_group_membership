How often and with whom is a group property; how is not

Figure 4. Temporal decomposition of social structure over six calendar quarters (2025Q1–2026Q2). (a) For each quantity, the share of variance sitting between groups rather than within a group over time, with a permutation test that shuffles group labels across group-quarters and so keeps quarter-to-quarter drift intact. Event rate per group-week is strongly a group property (ICC 0.66, and collar count does not predict it, p = 0.29). Event family composition is about half a group property (ICC 0.53, 0.47, 0.32; all p ≤ 0.001). Merge-scale composition is not (ICC 0.06–0.13, permutation p = 0.09–0.26), nor is within-group modularity (ICC 0.17). (b) The same merge-scale proportion quarter by quarter, one line per group; groups with five or more usable quarters are drawn heavy and labelled. Lapis traverses 0.30–0.76 across its own six quarters on 376 merges — a swing of 0.46, wider than the whole between-group spread. LapisSplinter is the exception, holding 0.48–0.62 over six quarters. (c) Empirical distribution of merge-partner set overlap between consecutive quarters, 74 group-quarter transitions. Mean Jaccard 0.61 against 0.15 for a null that redraws the same number of partners from those actually available that quarter — 4.0×. Groups keep their partners.

This corrects Figure 3. That figure tested between-group composition against a multinomial null in which each group drew from a fixed pooled distribution with no temporal structure. Panel (b) here shows why that null is too weak for merge scale: once quarter-to-quarter drift is retained, the between-group ordering is not distinguishable from shuffled labels. Figure 3's family-composition result stands; its merge-scale panel should be read as showing variation in the population, not differences between groups.
