01  /  Why the structure changed

Copper–Lilac is the tail of a distribution, not the spine of a paper

Two facts from the saved tables argue against a case-led paper. First, Copper–Lilac supplies 27,891 of 39,975 two-minute rows in the cross-dyad file — 69.8%. Any statistic pooled over those rows is largely a Copper–Lilac statistic, so cross-dyad claims must be weighted by dyad, not by bin.

Second, Copper–Lilac is the outlier rather than the exemplar. Every one of the twelve supported dyads sits below its composition expectation for close-range mixing, but the spread is wide, and Copper–Lilac sits almost at expectation while the rest sit far below it.

General result When two groups share space, close-range mixing is almost always far below what group composition alone would produce. Copper–Lilac is the rare record that approaches its own expectation — which makes it the demonstration of what full fusion looks like, at the top of a gradient, rather than the lead result.
Revised by Phase 3 — see the companion, Meeting versus mixing Separating discrete encounters from sustained association sharpens this. Copper–Lilac's encounters give a 5 m deficit of −0.339, tenth of twelve — inside the pack. Only its fused period reaches −0.033. So the outlier is a state, not a dyad: encounters barely mix everywhere (dyad-weighted −0.405 [−0.442, −0.367], 12 of 12 below expectation), and near-expectation mixing belongs to sustained association, which five dyads entered.

Persistence and permeability are simultaneous properties of one population, not competing descriptions of it.

Level 2 — Encounter and mixing are different processes at different scales

Structural encounter — shared spatial cluster

3,217

Detected close contact — current model response

117

positive dyad-days out of 56,128 candidates across 207 dyads — a 0.21% positive rate in 21 dyads, with 56,000 candidate days carrying zero eligible 2-minute bins.

The two numbers do not conflict; they measure different things. But it means the encounter response now feeding the ecological models is a rare-event indicator of detected close contact, not an indicator of groups meeting. That definitional gap is the largest structural problem in the project, and it is general — it depends on no single dyad.

Level 3 — A permeable population can retain internal structure

Candidate routes connecting groups: repeated dyadic encounters, individual departures (5,828 separation events; 71 dispersal segments from 17 animals), and within-group fission that redistributes companions (4,570 split events; 466 analysed events from 15 groups, preceding-proximity AUC 0.568, bootstrap 0.558–0.578, mean ARI 0.100).

Claim — not yet supported These routes generate a structured, non-random network among groups. This is the level most likely to justify a broad-audience venue and the level with the least completed work. State it as the paper's ambition; assert it only once time-respecting connectivity is quantified.
03  /  The unblocking decision

Define the encounter at the structural scale, then measure mixing inside it

Stage 1 — structural encounter. Two social units occupy a shared spatial cluster, detected independently of close-range contact, using the existing association_event_id and merge-scale machinery.

Stage 2 — mixing within the encounter. Retain all eligible fine-scale bins with a valid denominator, including bins with zero cross-group contact. Report a rate with observation, distinct from an undefined rate with no denominator.

The saved pipeline does the opposite: build_positive_episodes() filters to has_cross_edge == True before stitching, so the reported integration fraction describes only the contact-positive part of an episode. Every ecological coefficient inherits that conditioning.

Adopting the two-stage definition raises the stage-1 sample from 21 dyads to about 74, removes the rare-event pathology from the occurrence model, makes “shared space without mixing” a measured outcome rather than a missing row, and places Copper–Lilac on the same axis as every other dyad.

06  /  Continuation plan

Eight gated phases; no phase starts before its predecessor's gate is met

New work goes in outputs/general_structure_2026_09/. Nothing existing is overwritten. Phases 0–3 are the critical path and yield a defensible paper on their own.

Ph 0
Cohort ledger and definition sheet Critical path

A source-to-analysis ledger for every membership product — rows, animals, groups, date range, builder parameters, and which saved analyses consumed it — plus an event-ID crosswalk between the 1,924,104-row export and the legacy 1,703,133-row inventory source. One definitions sheet covering all seven rows above.

GateEvery saved result maps to exactly one cohort, and every quantity has one written definition with a worked example.
Ph 1
Opportunity table Critical path

Replace zero-filled candidate days with a dyad-window table carrying shared observation duration and the four explicit states. Validate against known examples, including the 747 Copper–Lilac days currently coded as zero encounters.

GateDyad-window counts reported per state; no state inferred from a missing row; supported negatives distinguishable from unobserved windows.
Ph 2
Two-stage encounter unit Critical path

Implement stage 1 and stage 2. Produce an event table with structural span, supported exposure and active-contact time as separate columns, plus one worked event showing which bins are retained, which are dropped, and why.

GateThe event table reproduces a hand-checked example, and stage-1 counts are reported per dyad with observation support.
Ph 3
The general mixing gradient First publishable result

One observation-aware metric across all supported dyads with origin labels preserved, weighted by dyad rather than by bin. Report cross-origin and within-origin rates alongside their ratio so a falling reference stays visible. Sensitivity: radius, same-bin timing tolerance, GPS error, partner availability, stable-collar subsets.

GateFigure 3 exists with per-dyad support and intervals, and the pooled statement is demonstrably not a restatement of one dyad.
Ph 4
Two-stage model with sound inference

Fit both stages on the repaired samples, with a likelihood matched to the response and uncertainty matched to repeated dyads, groups and animals. Use a shared group effect for unordered pairs rather than alphabetical group_a / group_b terms. Replace or rename the normal-approximation and conjugate-sampling fits; columns named p_value in the saved Bayesian tables are posterior sign proportions. Use genuinely preceding covariates, and separate between-dyad proximity from within-dyad change.

GateOne retained sample across all model comparisons; prior sensitivity and calibration reported; no effect attributed to an added predictor without a matched-sample comparison.
Ph 5
Symmetric ecological test

Fission and intergroup encounter share one environmental framework with predictions that can fail: scarcity fragments, abundance connects. Add the missing environmental model of fission, control for season and shared concentrated resources, and separate demographic size from collar coverage.

GateBoth processes modeled on the frozen cohort with the same covariates; the two-sided prediction supported or rejected in print.
Ph 6
Connectivity

Quantify time-respecting connections among groups from encounters and transfers, against an opportunity-adjusted null.

GateResult 5 is either quantified or explicitly withdrawn from the main text.
Ph 7
Case studies and supplement hygiene

Recast Copper–Lilac as the labeled endpoint of Phase 3, with phase-contrast uncertainty and alternative phase boundaries. Reconcile dispersal outcomes (71 segments from 17 animals versus 28 events from 9 animals; repeated segments; censoring). Fix two confirmed export faults: leaver bootstrap intervals mislabeled in compare_centrality_stayed_left.py, and outcome-based selection above 100 positive 5 m bins in plot_permanent_5m_colocation_cadence_filtered.py.

GateEvery supplementary claim states its selection rule; no figure selects on the measured outcome without saying so.

Where the headline is decided

Phase 5 decides whether the paper is structural — how much groups actually mix — or ecological — what makes boundaries open. Recommendation: build Phases 0–3 first and keep ecology as a test appended in Phase 5, rather than promising a mechanism the current denominators cannot support.

What to stop doing

About twenty single-purpose scripts target individual dyads and animals. They were useful for discovery; continuing to add them raises the reconciliation burden without moving any of the five results forward. Freeze them, and add new analysis only through the shared Phase 2 event table, so every dyad is measured the same way.
